Understanding Dental Crowns

A dental crown, sometimes called a tooth crown, is a custom made cap that fits over a tooth like a protective shell. It restores the original shape, size and strength of a tooth that has been weakened by decay, cracking, wear or a large filling. Crowns are also used after root canal treatment, to cover a misshapen or discolored tooth and to finish a dental implant. Because a crown surrounds the entire visible part of the tooth, it holds everything together and guards against further damage.

We offer several materials. A porcelain crown is admired for its lifelike glow and is a favorite for front teeth. A zirconia crown is exceptionally strong and resists chipping, which makes it well suited to molars and to patients who clench or grind. During your consultation we look at the location of the tooth, your bite, your goals and your budget before recommending the best option.

Understanding Dental Bridges

Missing teeth do more than leave a gap. Neighboring teeth can drift, your bite can shift and chewing can become uncomfortable. A dental bridge solves this by joining one or more artificial teeth to crowns placed on the teeth on either side of the space. A fixed dental bridge is cemented in place, so it feels stable and does not need to be taken out for cleaning. A missing tooth bridge can restore your smile, make eating easier and help keep your remaining teeth in their proper positions.

Not every gap calls for the same solution, so we take time to examine the supporting teeth, the gums and the bone before we recommend a bridge. Our goal is a result that looks natural, feels comfortable and holds up well to everyday use.

Frequently Asked Questions

Straight answers to the questions patients ask us most about dental crowns and bridges.

How long does a dental crown last?

With good brushing, flossing and regular checkups, a dental crown often lasts ten to fifteen years or longer. Habits such as grinding your teeth or chewing ice can shorten that time, so we may suggest a night guard when it makes sense.

Is getting a crown or bridge painful?

The treated area is numbed before work begins, so most patients feel pressure but not pain. Mild sensitivity for a few days afterward is common and usually fades quickly.

What is the difference between porcelain and zirconia crowns?

Porcelain crowns are prized for lifelike translucency, which makes them popular for front teeth. Zirconia crowns are extremely strong and resist chipping, which makes them a good choice for molars.

How many visits does a crown or fixed bridge require?

Most crowns and bridges take two visits. The first prepares the teeth and takes impressions, and the second places the finished restoration after a temporary is worn in between.

Can I eat normally with a dental bridge?

Yes. Once your fixed bridge is cemented and your bite is checked, you can return to a normal diet. It is wise to avoid very hard or sticky foods for the first few days.

What payment methods do you accept?

We accept cash and credit cards. Our team will review your treatment plan and costs with you before any work begins.

Dental Crowns

Dental crowns are one of the most trusted ways to protect and rebuild a damaged tooth. A crown covers the entire visible portion of the tooth, restoring its shape, strength and appearance while shielding what remains from further harm. Patients commonly need a crown after a large cavity, a crack, heavy wear or a root canal. Crowns can also support a bridge or cover a tooth that is misshapen or stained beyond what whitening can fix.

At our Boynton Beach office, we prepare the tooth with care, take precise impressions and fit a temporary crown while your permanent one is made. When it is ready, we check the bite, the color and the comfort before cementing it. With good home care, a well made crown can serve you for many years. Dental Bridges

Dental bridges replace one or more missing teeth by connecting artificial teeth to crowns on the natural teeth beside the gap. They restore your ability to chew, improve clarity of speech and bring back a complete, confident smile. Just as important, a bridge helps prevent the neighboring teeth from tilting into the open space, which can cause bite problems and make cleaning harder over time.

Our team examines the supporting teeth, gums and bone before recommending a bridge, since strong anchor teeth are essential for a lasting result. We then design a bridge that matches your natural color and fits comfortably with your bite. Bridges are crafted from durable materials such as porcelain and zirconia and are cemented in place for dependable everyday use. If you are living with a gap, schedule a consultation and learn how a bridge can help.

Tooth Crown

A tooth crown is the answer when a tooth is too damaged for a simple filling but still worth saving. Over the years, chewing forces, grinding and decay can leave a tooth with thin walls that are likely to fracture. A crown acts like a protective helmet. It holds the tooth together, spreads biting pressure evenly and keeps bacteria from reaching the vulnerable inner structure.

Many patients are surprised by how natural a modern tooth crown looks and feels. We shape each one to match the height and contour of your own teeth, so it blends in and does not interfere with how you bite. If you have a tooth that hurts when you chew, feels fragile or has a large old filling, an exam can tell you whether a crown would protect it. Early treatment often prevents more complex and costly problems later, so do not wait for a small crack to become a break.

Porcelain Crown

A porcelain crown is designed for patients who want a restoration that disappears into their smile. Porcelain reflects light much like natural enamel, so it can be matched closely to the color, brightness and translucency of the teeth beside it. That is why porcelain crowns are so popular for front teeth and other highly visible areas where appearance matters as much as function.

Beyond beauty, porcelain is gentle on the surrounding gum tissue and resists staining, so it keeps its look year after year. Our team selects the shade carefully in natural light and works with a skilled laboratory to create a crown with lifelike detail. We also check that the crown fits your bite so it feels comfortable from the first day. If you have a chipped, discolored or worn front tooth, a porcelain crown can restore both its strength and its good looks. Call us to discuss whether porcelain suits your needs.

Zirconia Crown

A zirconia crown is known for exceptional strength. Made from a dense ceramic material, zirconia stands up to the heavy forces of chewing and resists chips and cracks far better than many traditional options. That makes it a smart choice for molars, for patients who clench or grind their teeth and for anyone who wants a restoration with outstanding durability.

Modern zirconia is also more attractive than many people expect. Newer formulations offer improved translucency and can be shaded to blend with your natural teeth. Because zirconia is kind to opposing teeth and the gums, it is comfortable for long term wear. We design each zirconia crown with a precise digital fit, which helps the margins seal tightly against the tooth. If you want a long lasting crown for a back tooth or a tooth that works hard, ask us whether zirconia is right for you.

Dental Crown Restoration

Even the best crown does not last forever. Over time, a crown can chip, loosen, wear thin or develop decay at its edge. Dental crown restoration is the process of evaluating an existing crown and renewing it so the tooth underneath stays protected. Signs that a crown needs attention include sensitivity, a rough edge, a visible dark line at the gum, a change in your bite or a crown that feels loose.

During your visit, we examine the crown and the tooth beneath it and take images to check for hidden problems. In some cases a small repair is enough. In others, the best option is to remove the old crown, treat the tooth and place a new one with a better fit and updated materials. Acting early can save the underlying tooth and avoid a more extensive procedure. If an older crown is bothering you, schedule a checkup and let us help you restore comfort and confidence.

Missing Tooth Bridge

Losing a tooth can happen because of decay, injury, gum disease or an extraction. Whatever the cause, leaving the space empty often leads to bigger problems. Neighboring teeth begin to lean, the opposing tooth can drift downward and chewing may become uneven. A missing tooth bridge closes that gap with a natural looking replacement that is supported by the healthy teeth on either side.

Treatment is straightforward. We prepare the anchor teeth, take impressions and place a temporary bridge so you are never without a tooth. Once your custom bridge is ready, we fit it carefully, check your bite and show you how to keep it clean. Patients often tell us they are surprised at how quickly they get used to it. If you have been hiding your smile because of a gap, a missing tooth bridge may restore your comfort and your confidence. Call our Boynton Beach office to learn more.

Fixed Dental Bridge

A fixed dental bridge is cemented permanently to your supporting teeth, so it stays put when you eat, speak and sleep. Unlike a removable partial denture, there are no clasps, no adhesives and nothing to take out at night. Many patients prefer this stable, natural feel, and it often helps them chew a wider variety of foods with confidence.

A fixed bridge is built from sturdy materials such as porcelain fused to metal or all ceramic zirconia, depending on where it will sit and how much force it will bear. We carefully plan the span of the bridge and the strength of the anchor teeth so the result lasts. Cleaning beneath a bridge takes a little practice, and we gladly show you how to use floss threaders and interdental brushes. With regular checkups and good habits, a fixed dental bridge can give you many years of reliable service.

Tooth Restoration

Tooth restoration covers the many ways a dentist can repair a tooth that has been damaged by decay, trauma or wear. Depending on how much of the natural tooth remains, restoration might involve a filling, an inlay, a crown or a bridge. Our approach is to preserve as much healthy tooth structure as possible and choose the least invasive option that will still give a durable result.

A restored tooth should do more than look good in a mirror. It should handle everyday chewing, feel comfortable against your tongue and cheek and protect against future decay. We examine your bite and consider how each repaired tooth will work with the rest of your mouth. Restoring a damaged tooth early often prevents the pain, infection and tooth loss that can follow when problems are ignored. If you have a chipped, cracked or broken tooth, call us so we can restore it promptly and comfortably.
